# ChordFlow — User Guide

*This mirrors the in-app Guide (Settings → Guide). If you're using the app, read it there.*

A jazz chord-progression practice tool. It deals you changes, shows you a
keyboard voicing for each one, and plays them back so you can read, comp and hear along.

## Start here

- Hit **New** (the transport bar) for a fresh progression, or open **Library** for a standard.
- Press **Play**. The chart highlights the chord as it sounds.
- Tap any chord box to see its voicing on the piano.

## The five tabs

- **Pads** — tap a chord to hear it, any time. Good for ear checks.
- **Voicing** — the piano. Which notes, which hand, what it's really sounding.
- **Dictionary** — look up any chord type and hear its voicings.
- **Library** — standards and exercises, plus progressions you've saved.
- **Settings** — configuration, and this guide.

## The two settings that change the most

**Complexity** (Song) sets the harmonic vocabulary — from
*Simple Triads* up through *Jazz 7ths*, *Extended* and
*Altered*. Start lower than you think; the chart gets busy fast.

**Ensemble** (Sound) decides who plays what:

- *App plays both hands* — Auto, Bass root, Shells, Two-hand rootless. Watch and copy.
- *App plays left hand* — LH comp. You take the melody with your right.
- *You comp* — With a bassist / App plays bass. The app holds down the low end and leaves the chords to you.

The chip beside the chord name in the Voicing tab cycles these without coming back here.

## Reading the piano

Left-hand notes and right-hand notes are coloured separately and listed above the keyboard.
If a **Sounding:** line appears, the voicing differs from the chord symbol on the
chart — usually richer (a Cmaj7 voiced with the 9 sounds as Cmaj9). It will also tell you when a
voicing *leaves out* the 3rd or 7th and is relying on your ear or a bassist to imply them.

Tap the voicing name to cycle alternatives for the same chord. The list scrolls — there are
usually more than fit on screen.

## Practice tools (Practice tab)

- **12 Keys** — transposes at each loop, so you drill all keys.
- **Tempo ramp** — nudges the tempo up every loop.
- **Symbols** — hides chord names so you read from numerals. Flashcard mode.

## Keyboard shortcuts

`Space` play/pause · `R` new progression · `V` voicing panel ·
`←``→` tempo ±5 · `[``]` previous/next chord

## One honest note

The voicings here are drawn from standard jazz vocabulary — rootless
shapes, guide-tone shells, quartals, upper structures. But the specific register and note
choices are largely the app's own, and most have not been checked against transcriptions of
actual players. Treat it as a practice tool rather than an authority, and trust a real
recording over it. The project tracks exactly what rests on what in
`CLAIMS.md`.
