# ChordFlow — User Guide

*Mirrors the in-app Guide (Settings → Guide). If you're using the app, read it there.*

A jazz chord-progression practice tool. It deals you changes, shows a
keyboard voicing for each one, and plays them back so you can read, comp and hear along.

> **Read this first.** This app was vibe-coded — built fast
by an AI, steered by a musician whose training is in contemporary classical, not jazz. It is a
**very loose approximation** of idiomatic jazz keyboard playing. The chord shapes
are real jazz vocabulary, but the specific voicings, registers and choices are mostly the
app's own invention and have *not* been checked against how players actually play.
It is a drilling tool, not a teacher and not an authority. There are real sources at the
bottom of this guide — use them.

## Start here

- Press **New** (the circular arrow ↻) for a fresh progression, or open
**Library** for a standard.
- Press **Play** (the triangle ▶, the gold button). The chart highlights each
chord as it sounds.
- **On a phone or tablet: take it off silent.** iPhones and iPads mute web
audio with the hardware ring/silent switch, so on silent the app looks like it is running
perfectly and playing nothing. Turn the ringer on and bring the volume up.
- Tap any chord box to see its voicing on the piano.

## The transport bar (bottom)

- **Top** (bar + double triangle ⏮) — jump back to chord 1. Works while
playing without stopping.
- **Prev** (triangle pointing left, bar on its left ◀|) — step back one chord.
- **Play / Pause** (triangle ▶, gold) — starts and pauses the loop.
- **Stop** (square ■) — stop and rewind to the top.
- **Next** (triangle pointing right, bar on its right ▶|) — step forward one chord.
Stepping while stopped also plays the chord, so you can walk a progression at your own pace.
- **Tempo** (the number, e.g. 120) — tap to open a BPM slider.
- **New** (circular arrow ↻) — generate a new random progression.
- **Flavor** (droplet 💧) — cycles Off → Subtle → Bold. Adds borrowed
harmony to *newly generated* progressions: minor iv and v, backdoor ♭VII7,
chromatic mediants. Dots on the button show the level.

## The chord strip

Roman numerals above, chord symbols below, with beat dots showing where you are. A gold bar
tracks progress through the loop; the counters at the right show bar and loop number.

- **Tap a chord** to select it and see its voicing. **Tap again**
to cycle to a different voicing of the same chord.
- **Sub** badges under a chord open its substitution tray.
- Chords tinted differently are **borrowed** — from outside the home key.
- An **as written** chip means a library tune is in its original key with its
original chord qualities, rather than regenerated.

## Tabs

- **Pads** — one pad per chord, tap to hear it any time. The
*Trigger* button switches between *One-shot* (a tap rings out) and
*Hold* (sounds only while your finger is down, like a damper pedal). Polyphonic —
hold several at once.
- **Voicing** — the piano and what your hands would do. See below.
- **Dictionary** — every chord type the app knows, by category. Pick a root,
pick a chord, and every voicing is listed with its left- and right-hand notes; tap one to
hear it. Some chords have a "Think of it as…" shortcut. Substitution chips at the bottom
sound the substitute chord and jump you to it.
- **Library** — standards, exercises, and progressions you save. Loading a
standard opens it in its original key.
- **Settings** — everything below, plus this guide.

## Reading the voicing panel

Left-hand and right-hand notes are listed separately and coloured to match the keyboard
below, with octave numbers (C3 is middle-C's octave here).

- The line under the piano names the voicing — tap the chord again to cycle.
- **Sounding:** appears when the voicing differs from the chart symbol.
Usually richer (a Cmaj7 voiced with the 9 sounds as Cmaj9). It will also warn when a voicing
*leaves out* the 3rd or 7th and is relying on your ear, or a bassist, to imply them.
- The **A/B** button flips between the original changes and your substituted
ones, so you can hear what a sub actually did.
- The chip beside the chord name cycles **Ensemble** without leaving the tab.
- The **sub tray** under the piano lists substitutions. Tap once to hear it,
tap again to apply. Applying during playback runs it as a two-loop trial that reverts itself
unless you tap again to keep it. An *Undo* chip appears after any change.

## Settings — Song

- **Key** and **Mode** (major/minor) — transposes the current
progression rather than replacing it.
- **Complexity** — the harmonic vocabulary:
*Simple Triads*; *7th Chords (Strict)* chord tones only;
*Root-Shell-Pretty* root, guide tones, one colour note;
*Jazz 7ths* the default; *Extended* 9ths/11ths/13ths;
*Altered &amp; Complex*. Start lower than you think.
- **Bars** — progression length, 2–8.
- **Beats/Chord** — how long each chord lasts: 2, 4 or 8 beats.

## Settings — Sound

- **Metronome** — click on every beat, accented on beat 1.
- **Comping** — rhythm feel: *Block chords* (one hit, held),
*Charleston*, *Bossa*, *Half-note pulse*.
- **Swing 8ths** — swings the off-beat eighths of whichever comping pattern
is playing.
- **Ensemble** — who plays what. *App plays both hands*: Auto (picks a
left hand per chord and voice-leads it), Bass root, Root + guide tones (root, 3rd and 7th),
Shells (the 3rd and 7th alone — a bassist covers the root), Two-hand rootless.
*App plays left hand*: LH comp — close voicings moving through inversions, so you take
the melody. *You comp*: With a bassist (app drops the root), App plays bass (app holds
roots, you play the chords).
- **Range** — constrain every voicing to one 3-octave C–C window, for a
37-key controller.
- **Backing bass** — sustains a bass root under playback when the left hand
isn't playing one.
- **Octave roots** — doubles a lone left-hand bass root at the octave.

## Settings — Practice

- **12 Keys** — transposes at each loop, by fourths or chromatically, so you
drill a progression through all keys.
- **Tempo ramp** — adds BPM every loop. Combine with 12 Keys to make it hurt.
- **Symbols** — hides chord names so you read from roman numerals only.
Flashcard mode.

## Saving

*My Progressions* in the Library tab saves whatever is loaded — key, mode,
complexity, substitutions and all — and restores it exactly. You can rename, delete, and
export/import as JSON to move things between devices. Everything is stored in your browser
only; nothing is uploaded.

## Keyboard shortcuts

`Space` play/pause · `R` new progression · `V` voicing panel ·
`←``→` tempo ±5 · `[``]` previous/next chord

## Where to actually learn this

This app can drill your reading and get changes under your fingers. It cannot teach you the
idiom, because it does not reliably contain the idiom. The real sources:

**A teacher.** Genuinely the first answer. Everything below is a supplement.

**Listen — this music is an aural tradition, and the recordings are the primary
source.** There is no score behind them.

- *Bebop left hand* — **Bud Powell** (*The Amazing Bud Powell*),
**Thelonious Monk**, **Duke Jordan**, **Tadd Dameron**.
- *Rootless comping* — **Bill Evans** (*Portrait in Jazz*),
**Red Garland** (the Miles Davis *Workin'/Relaxin'/Steamin'* records),
**Wynton Kelly**.
- *Modal and quartal* — **Bill Evans** on Miles Davis,
*Kind of Blue* (1959); **McCoy Tyner** with Coltrane
(*A Love Supreme*).
- *Solo and stride* — **Art Tatum**, **Teddy Wilson**,
**Erroll Garner**.
- *Modern* — **Kenny Barron**, **Fred Hersch**,
**Brad Mehldau**.

**Read and play the transcriptions.** Hal Leonard publishes note-for-note
books — the *Bud Powell Omnibook* and *Bill Evans Omnibook for Piano* are the
direct answer to "what did they actually play with the left hand." Treat these the way a
classical player treats a scholarly edition.

**Books.** Mark Levine, *The Jazz Piano Book* (the standard modern
text) and *The Jazz Theory Book*; Bill Dobbins, *A Creative Approach to Jazz Piano
Harmony*. **Barry Harris**'s workshops are widely recorded and are a
teaching tradition in their own right.

**Play with people.** None of this transfers until you comp for a soloist who
reacts to what you do.

> If something here contradicts a recording or a teacher, they are right
and the app is wrong. The project tracks exactly which of its claims rest on real sources and
which are invention in `CLAIMS.md` — at last count, 2 of 142 voicings cited an
actual recording.
